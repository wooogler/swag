SCORE · LLM-as-a-judge reliability

How steady is the judge?

A test–retest audit of the 5-level rating behind SCORE's starter-set subtypes — the same student request scored eight times — and what changed once the judge was told which part of a message is the student's request and which part is pasted material.

01

What the Material/Request fix changed

The judge used to receive the raw student message and guess what was a request. It now receives the deterministic Material/Request split reconstructed from the edit log, and is told never to invent a request out of pasted material. Both arms were measured identically — same 20 queries, 8 repeats. The improvement lands exactly where the fix aims, and nowhere else.

How far do the eight runs swing?

Every one of the 520 (query × subtype) judgments was rated 8 times. This is the spread between the highest and lowest of those 8 verdicts on the 4-step ordinal scale — the share that never moved, moved one step, two, or flipped end-to-end.

03

The instability map

Each square is one (query × subtype) judgment. Darker = the 8 runs disagreed more. A red outline marks the cells where the wobble crossed the clearly_in line that decides assignment — the flips that change behavior. Instability is sparse and clustered, not spread evenly.

04

Which subtypes are shaky?

Mean run-to-run agreement per starter-set subtype (share of the 8 runs landing on the modal verdict, averaged over the 20 queries). Narrow, lexically-cued subtypes are rock-solid; broad or overlapping ones wobble. A ▸ flags subtypes whose clearly_in decision flipped on ≥10% of queries.

05

Ambiguity, or noise?

For each remaining decision-flip we asked three fresh, independent judges — blind to the production runs — to each pick the single most defensible rating. Where that panel also splits, the query is genuinely ambiguous and the wobble is expected; the fix belongs in intent design, not the model. Where the panel concentrates but production waffled, that is model noise.

    How this was measured

    The judge is the exact production path — rateMessageIntents → callModel — with the real starter-set definitions, prior-context strategy, strict 4-level schema, and default reasoning effort. SCORE's models are reasoning models that accept no temperature, top_p or seed, so this measures the judge's inherent run-to-run nondeterminism — the floor you get in production, not a temperature artifact. Because that floor is stochastic, the before/after arms are read together with a control: subtypes on messages containing no pasted material cannot be touched by the fix, so their movement (±2 flips) is the sampling-noise band.
